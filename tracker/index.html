Setting up the camera
  1. Put the phone on a tripod behind the bowler's stumps, at least 150 cm high, level, and a little to one side so the bowler doesn't block the ball. The whole pitch must be in frame and the phone must not move. Use 60 fps.
  2. Tap Calibrate pitch and drag the three markers onto the bottom and the top of the batter's stumps and the bottom of the stumps at your end. A magnifier appears while you drag. Then enter the phone's height from the ground. If the batter hides their stumps, move the phone a little to one side. Do it again whenever the phone moves.
  3. Set the field on the Field tab, tap Start session and bowl. Each ball is picked up and saved with a short video. Tap End session to see the summary and watch every ball again.

What to expect

One camera can't see depth directly, so the ball's path is rebuilt from physics: gravity, the bounce on the calibrated pitch, and the bat impact. In testing on simulated balls that gives ball speed to within about 4% on a typical delivery (worse for yorkers and at 30 fps), swing to a few centimetres and turn to about half a degree. Real footage will be rougher: blur and shadows both hurt.

Bat speed is estimated from how fast the ball left the bat, not measured directly. Spin is reported as turn off the pitch in degrees; a phone camera cannot count revolutions. Shot results come from carrying the measured shot out over your field with drag, bounce and roll.
