Setting up the camera
  1. Put the phone on a tripod behind the bowler's arm (or behind the keeper), at least 150 cm high (higher is fine) and a little to one side so the bowler doesn't block the ball. The whole pitch must be in frame and the phone must not move. You don't enter the height: it is worked out when you calibrate, and the app warns you if the camera is below 150 cm.
  2. Tap Calibrate pitch. Either drag the four corners onto the corners of the pitch (the stump line at each end, at the pitch's edges), or switch to Use the stumps and mark the base and top of the middle stump at each end. A magnifier appears while you drag. Pitch corners are the more accurate of the two; use the stumps when the pitch edges aren't marked, as in most nets.
  3. Set the field on the Field tab, then bowl. Each ball is picked up automatically.

What to expect

One camera can't see depth directly, so the ball's path is rebuilt from physics: gravity, the bounce on the calibrated pitch, and the bat impact. In testing on simulated balls that gives ball speed to within about 4% on a typical delivery (worse for yorkers and at 30 fps), swing to a few centimetres and turn to about half a degree. Real footage will be rougher: blur and shadows both hurt.

Bat speed is estimated from how fast the ball left the bat, not measured directly. Spin is reported as turn off the pitch in degrees; a phone camera cannot count revolutions. Shot results come from carrying the measured shot out over your field with drag, bounce and roll.
